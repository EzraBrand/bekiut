# Twenty additional places

Reviewed 15 October 2026 against the linked English and Hebrew Wikipedia articles and the existing Talmudic gazetteer.

Added Usha, Shefaram, Beitar, Haifa, Sidon, Gaza, Antipatris, Nisibis, Sikhnei, Gush Halav, Yodfat, Gamla, Ein Gedi, Kefar Sava, Meron, Pamyas, Emmaus, Carthage, Ctesiphon, and Puteoli. All have exact source-list membership and Hebrew search aliases.

Selection favors rabbinic centers, recognizable ancient cities, and locatable sites. This is a qualitative selection, **not a corpus-frequency ranking**. Some frequently mentioned places were already present; the list also includes recognizable places with fewer occurrences.

Each new record exposes a reviewed Hebrew Wikipedia identification reference. Disambiguation and nonexistent pages were replaced with actual articles, particularly for Gamla, Ein Gedi, Banias, Yodfat, and Pozzuoli. The existing references UI displays these links without introducing a separate link mechanism.

Coordinates follow the cited English article except Gaza and ancient Yodfat, where the Hebrew article supplies the coordinate. The English Yodfat article's leading coordinate belongs to the modern village; it was deliberately not used. Kefar Sava follows the later Kafr Saba site rather than the modern city center. City-level points are approximate; differences between language editions' city-center coordinates do not imply distinct ancient identifications. Ctesiphon remains separate from Mehoza on the opposite bank.

Wikipedia is an orientation source, not specialist validation. Similar names, modern boundaries, and tomb traditions are not treated as settled historical geography. Corpus filters continue to mean membership in the supplied name lists, not a claim that a site is absent from the other corpus.
