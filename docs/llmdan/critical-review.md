# Review of the two draft additions

## A. Bava Batra 164b draft

**1. Medium: The lexical recurrence is treated as evidence of thematic unity.**
- **Problem:** "Such is the custom of this nation" (כך מנהגה של אומה זו) does appear in both 164b.5 (Zunin) and 164b.8 (Rav Hoshaya). The draft then says this repetition "places the archon problem within" a single problem of documentary interpretation. A shared formula does not show that the redactor intended that framing.
- **Correction:** "The same formula introduces both explanations. Read together, they show that a date can depend on a local convention. The text does not say whether the two discussions were designed as a pair."

**2. Medium: Zunin's convention is mischaracterized.**
- **Problem:** The draft calls it "a foreign convention for counting regnal years." In context (164b.1, 164b.5), the convention concerns how years are counted in a tied document (*mekushar*). The text does not call it foreign.
- **Correction:** Describe it as the tied-document counting convention attributed to "this nation." Do not call it foreign.

**3. Low: The closing sentence of the first paragraph is generic.**
- **Problem:** "Administrative knowledge is something a legal interpreter may need to acquire from another person" adds little beyond 164b.6, "after he heard it from Zunin, he held it."
- **Correction:** Cut the sentence or merge it into the preceding one.

**4. Medium: The Greek comparison is mismatched.**
- **Problem:** The draft compares the term to the eponymous archon. That magistrate held a one-year office. The sugya assumes a multi-year rule (דאריך מלכותיה, "his reign extended") and a deposed-then-reinstated ruler. The comparison may therefore obscure rather than illuminate. The LSJ sense "II" is also cited without verification in the packet.
- **Correction:** Either drop the comparison, or say it is analogous only in that a title marks the year. State that the sugya's ruler holds a reign (מלכות), not an annual magistracy. Verify the LSJ sense number before citing it.

**5. Low-medium: The status of Rabbi Yirmeya's reply is overstated.**
- **Problem:** The Aramaic is ההוא ״ארכן דיגון״ קראו ליה, past tense: "they called that one archon digon." "Would refer," and the reading as a general rule, come from the Davidson translation. The draft's claim that "the title's form distinguishes continued service from renewed appointment" treats that rule as given.
- **Correction:** Note that the reply may describe what such a ruler was called, or report a specific case. Frame the distinction as "on this reading."

**6. Low: The first objection is a wordplay, and the "Aramaic or Hebrew term" note is a gloss.**
- **Problem:** The objection reads ארכן as אריך ("extended"). Davidson's "Aramaic or Hebrew term" is an explanatory gloss.
- **Correction:** Say the objection rereads ארכן as אריך, "his reign extended." Do not present the language explanation as text.

**7. Low: Transliteration and wording are inconsistent.**
- **Problem:** The text vocalizes הֵינָא (*heina*), and the post uses *hena*/*heina*. The draft's "hina" matches neither. Also, Sumakhos's "one" is a legal ruling about the number of terms, not a gloss of the word.
- **Correction:** Use *heina*, and write "Sumakhos rules that *heina* means one term."
- **What to keep:** The critique of "henagon" is correct and should stay.

**8. Low-medium: The "exact four" reading is an inference.**
- **Problem:** 164b.12 derives four walls (two plus two). It does not itself address the pentagon. The exclusion comes from the preceding ruling. Shapes beyond five sides are not addressed.
- **Correction:** "Read together, the ruling's exclusion of the pentagon and the four-wall derivation suggest four is treated as a requirement, not a minimum, at least for the shapes listed."

**9. Low: The Tosefta parallels need a caution.**
- **Problem:** The draft does not cite the Tosefta, but editors should know the printed parallels differ substantially from the Bavli:
  - In Tosefta Nazir 1:2, Sumakhos gives טטרגין, דינין, דריגין, with different values.
  - Tosefta Negaim 6:3 lists דוגין נורגים ופנטניז, has no tetragon clause, and includes a beams/pillars distinction.
  - Mishnah Negaim 12:1 has only round, טריגון, and מרובע.
- **Correction:** If the post retains the Davidson citations ("Tosefta Nazir 1:2," "Nega'im 6:3"), call them parallels, not identical texts.

**10. Low: Part of the draft repeats the overview.**
- **Problem:** The second paragraph largely restates the post's summary of the two objections.
- **Correction:** Compress it to the new point, whether the reply is a general rule or a specific report (see #5).

## B. Shabbat 156b draft

**1. High (correct and important; strengthen it): Who was worried?**
- **What the draft gets right:** The Aramaic is הוה דאיג, masculine, so Rabbi Akiva is the one worried. Both Davidson and the post render it "She was very worried." The draft is right that her knowledge of the prediction cannot be inferred.
- **Correction:** State plainly that the post's sentence "She was very worried (דאיג)" mistranslates the form. It should read "He [Rabbi Akiva] was very worried." The post's "unknowingly" should also be softened: the text does not say whether she knew.

**2. Medium: Translation glosses must not be treated as text in the Akiva story.**
- **Problem:** The following are Davidson additions:
  - "from her hair"
  - "into a hole"
  - "for safekeeping"
  - "knocked" (the Aramaic קרא אבבא means "called at the door")

  The draft mostly avoids them, but "heard a poor man at the door" goes further than the text. The daughter reports that *nobody* heard him. That she did is only implied by her acting.
- **Correction:** "A poor man called at the door; no one heard him; she rose and gave him the portion her father had given her."

**3. Medium: The Shmuel story contains unsupported descriptions.**
- **Problem:**
  - Calling the man "the worker" is unsupported. He is only one of "these people going to the lake."
  - "The astrologers' claims" is inexact for that story. The predictor is Ablat, and the text does not label his method.
- **Correction:** Use "the man" or "the traveler," and "the predictions."

**4. High: The final story is overread.**
- **Problem:** "An ongoing susceptibility that requires continued discipline and divine assistance" goes beyond the narrative.
  - It is a single episode.
  - Nothing is said about his later conduct or about prayer's effect.
  - The Gemara gives no explanation of how the story supports "there is no *mazal* for Israel."
  - The explanatory last sentence in Davidson ("Apparently, he had an inborn inclination… overcome… with proper education and prayer") is editorial. The post reproduces it as though it were translation, and its parenthetical summary repeats it.
- **Correction:** Note that the story's evidentiary function is left implicit. It appears to imply that the head-covering restrained an inclination the lapse exposed. Name the Davidson sentence as a gloss, and recommend the post mark it as such. Replace "ongoing… continued" with "the episode suggests the restraint depended on the practice."

**5. Low: One detail of the final story is omitted.**
- **Problem:** "She tells him to cover his head" leaves out that she did not *allow* him to uncover it (לא שבקתיה). That enforcement is relevant to the "conditional restraint" reading.
- **Correction:** Add it.

**6. Low: The Tobit comparison falls outside the packet.**
- **Problem:** Tobit 12:8–9 is a reasonable comparator and is properly hedged against dependence. It cannot be checked against the packet, and it is tangential to this passage.
- **Correction:** Verify the verse wording, or cut it.

**7. Low: The draft does not flag the gloss in "righteousness."**
- **Problem:** The draft uses JPS's "righteousness," while the derasha applies צדקה to charitable acts. The Talmud also does not specify Proverbs 10:2 versus 11:4.
- **Correction:** Gloss צדקה as charity in the derasha's sense. The note on both verses is good; keep it.

**8. Low: The first paragraph partly repeats the overview.**
- **Correction:** Trim it to the corroborated-danger point and the caution against a uniform anti-astrology reading. That caution is accurate: Rabbi Ḥanina (156a.12) holds there is *mazal* for Israel.

**9. Medium: The bibliography is not fully verified.**
- **Problem:** The Gardner chapter's year (2007 vs. 2008), page range, and the claimed pp. 335–336 discussion of a Palestinian parallel cannot be confirmed from the packet. The draft discloses partial reading.
- **Correction:** Before publication, resolve the imprint year and confirm the pages against the printed volume. Keep the scope limitation.

## Remaining caveats

- The reviewed text is the William Davidson vocalized edition only. No manuscript variants were checked, and none are asserted here. This matters for the Greek terms and for דאיג.
- The draft's assertions are accurate where they track the Aramaic: the shared "מאי עבדת" question, the sermon on "וצדקה תציל ממות," the absence of a rescue or sermon in the third story, and the fact that the Aramaic does not specify ownership of the tree.
- Greek etymologies are limited to the identifications the drafts imply: *heina*/ἕν(α), *archōn*. The post's -hedron/πάρεδρος aside was not under review.
- Agreement between the draft and the post or the translation does not confirm accuracy. Several shared errors originate in the Davidson glosses.
