Consult and hyperlink the relevant Hebrew Wikipedia articles when researching additions to the Places page, alongside other sources.

**Why:** The user noted that Hebrew entries are often better for this subject.

**How to apply:** Inspect the actual article, distinguish ancient sites from modern towns, and avoid disambiguation links. Do not assume differing city-center coordinates indicate a disputed ancient identification.

Keep generic accuracy disclaimers in the page-level explanation, not repeated in individual entries. Retain genuinely place-specific identification uncertainties.

**Why:** The user finds repeated generic disclaimers excessive for this non-specialist map.

**How to apply:** Favor concise historical descriptions; do not add boilerplate about exact buildings or ancient boundaries to each record. The user reiterated that distinctions should be phrased positively (e.g. the city as opposed to the empire), rather than hedging about everything a record does not mean.

Regional toponyms may be included as explicitly labeled regional reference points.

**Why:** The user requested Cappadocia, expanding the original settlement-only selection.

**How to apply:** Clearly name regions as regions and describe what the point represents, such as a capital or an identifiable location within the region.

Keep identification certainty independent of coordinate precision, and make region selection filter entries as well as moving the map.

**Why:** The user noted that an approximate city-level pin for Jerusalem must not imply uncertainty about Jerusalem's identity, and expects region buttons to filter the list.

**How to apply:** Treat well-identified cities as established even with broad pin precision. Use curated geographic membership rather than inferring historical regions from a viewport rectangle.

Selecting a map place must visibly reveal its information.

**Why:** User feedback reported not discovering the information below the map until scrolling manually.

**How to apply:** Do not rely on invisible focus changes or instructions alone when details are off-screen; retain a clear transition to the selected entry.
